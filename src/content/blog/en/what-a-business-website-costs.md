Most answers to this question are useless on purpose. "It depends" protects the person saying it. So here are the numbers I actually quote, what moves them, and what nobody tells you until the invoice arrives.

## The ranges

- **Landing page — 500 to 900 €.** One page, one goal: a form, a call, a booking. Copy, design, mobile, basic technical SEO, analytics.
- **Corporate website — 1,200 to 2,500 €.** Several sections, a service catalogue, contact form, structured data, a blog if you want one.
- **Online store — 2,500 to 6,000 €.** Everything above plus catalogue, cart, payment, tax and shipping rules, and order handling.
- **Second language — 400 to 900 €** on top, per language.
- **Redesign and migration of an existing site — 800 to 2,000 €.**

A range is not indecision. It means the final number depends on scope, and scope is settled before anything is signed. Whoever gives you a single figure before understanding what you need is either guessing or planning to bill the difference later.

## What actually moves the number

**Content.** This is the biggest one, and it is almost never in the quote. If you hand over texts and photos, a site moves fast. If someone has to write the texts for you, that is a separate job with its own cost — and it is the job that decides whether the site works, not the design.

**Number of distinct page types.** Ten pages built from the same template cost barely more than one. Three pages that each look different cost like three designs, because they are three designs.

**Integrations.** A contact form is nothing. A form that writes into your CRM, a booking system with real availability, a payment gateway with invoicing — each of those is real work, and each can fail on its own.

**Languages.** Not the translation itself, which is the cheap part. The expensive part is that every language multiplies the URLs, needs correct `hreflang`, its own sitemap entries and its own search-engine setup. A badly built multilingual site ranks worse than a good single-language one.

## The three costs quotes leave out

**1. Hosting and domain.** Small, but not zero, and recurring. A domain is 10 to 15 € a year. Hosting for a static site can be free and for a site with a real backend starts around 5 to 20 € a month. Anyone quoting "hosting included forever" is including one year.

**2. Maintenance.** A site is not furniture. Dependencies get security patches, integrations change their APIs, certificates expire, and forms break silently — you only find out through the enquiries that never arrive. Plans start at 49 € a month. If you would rather not pay it, fine, but then decide *who* checks the site and *how often*, and write it down. "Whenever we notice" means "when a customer tells us it has been down for a week".

**3. Changes after launch.** Every site gets them, because you only really see what you need once it is live. Either you budget for a bundle of hours or you will negotiate each change under pressure. Hours run 35 to 60 € depending on volume.

## What it costs when it goes wrong

The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest project. Three things I keep seeing:

- **A site nobody can update.** Built on something exotic, or with the content hardcoded. Every text change becomes a paid job. Over three years that costs more than the difference you saved.
- **No technical SEO from day one.** No sitemap, no structured data, no canonical URLs, search console never set up. It is an hour of work at the start and a rebuild six months later, when the question "why don't we show up on Google" finally gets asked.
- **No access to your own stuff.** The domain registered in the developer's name, the hosting in their account, the code nowhere you can reach. Ask for this before you pay the first invoice: the domain in your name, the code in a repository you own, the hosting in an account you control. A professional hands these over without blinking.
